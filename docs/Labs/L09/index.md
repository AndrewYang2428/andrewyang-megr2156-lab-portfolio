## Process Research:

In one or two paragraphs, explain how laser cutting works and why it is a viable manufacturing process. Compare it with FDM printing on at least two of the following: speed, cost, material options, geometric limits.

For this project, we were required to use the laser cutter to create a design. Laser cutting is a manufacturing process that uses a highly focused beam of light (a laser) to cut, shape, or engrave materials with high precision. The laser generates enough energy to melt, burn, or vaporize material along a specific path, creating the desired shape. Laser cutting is a viable manufacturing process because it offers high precision, fast production, material efficiency, and the ability to produce complex shapes with minimal additional machining. These advantages make it useful in mechanical engineering, automotive manufacturing, aerospace, and product development. There are some differences between Laser Cutting and FDM Printing, which include, speed, cost, and geometric limits. The Laser Cutter is faster than the FMD Printer because the FMD Printer builds the part layer by layer instead of cutting out flat 2D parts at a time. The difference in cost can vary depending on the type of the material that you plan on using in the Laser Cutter. For the FMD Printer, the cost of equipment tends to be pretty low, which allows for small scale prototyping and individual parts. The geometric limits are straight-forward between this two equipment's. The Laser Cutter is best suited for 2D shapes, while the FDM Printers are able to print in 3D but the limitations on this are the overhangs and the supports. Laser cutting is a viable manufacturing process when producing flat components that require precision, speed, and consistent dimensions. In contrast, FDM 3D printing is more suitable for creating complex three-dimensional parts and prototypes without specialized cutting tools. The best process depends on the component's geometry, material requirements, production volume, and budget.
