## Analyze

Step 1: Measure your material. Anchor: Henry Maudslay. In the early 1800s, Maudslay built a bench micrometer he used to hold his workshop to his own standard of accuracy. His principle: precision begins with measurement. Never trust a nominal thickness. Sheet labeled "1/8 inch" rarely measures 1/8 inch.

Measure your sheet with calipers at a minimum of 3 locations.
Record each value and the mean. Use the mean as your thickness variable.
Step 2: Measure the kerf. Anchor: W. Edwards Deming. Deming taught that every process varies, and that you cannot improve a process until you measure it. Kerf is the material the laser burns away, and you must measure it, not guess it.

Choose a square size, D, between 15 mm and 30 mm. Record the value you chose.
Design and cut one D × D square kerf coupon.
Measure the square with calipers in both the X and Y directions.
Kerf = D − measured width. Record both values and note any difference between X and Y.
Step 3: Run a fit test. Callback: Taguchi. This is a small parameter study, the same method you used for infill in Week 2.

Design a fit coupon with one tab and 3 slots at 3 different clearances (tight, target, loose), based on your measured thickness and kerf.
Cut and test it. Rate each fit and photograph the results.

## Decide

Step 4: Choose your clearance.

Select the clearance that gives the most reliable fit, and explain why.
Enter it in your Parameter Table (below). Your final CAD must use this value.

## Communicate

Step 5: Cut and assemble your final kit. Record the cut on video and log your settings in the Laser Process Record.

Step 6: Document. Complete your portfolio using the headers in Note #2.
