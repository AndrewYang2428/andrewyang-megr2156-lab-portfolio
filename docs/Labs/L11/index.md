## Research:

### Spiral Design Process:
Research the Spiral Design Process and describe the attributes of each category (e.g., planning, risk analysis, engineering, evaluation).
Explain how this process can guide your own project iteration and improvement.

The Spiral Design Process provides a structured way to approach engineering problems and continuously improve a design. It teaches me that the first design is not necessarily the best design and that testing, evaluating, and making adjustments are important parts of engineering. I can use this process in my own projects to make better design decisions, identify potential failures earlier, and develop solutions that meet the required specifications. It also helps me understand that engineering is an ongoing process of learning and improvement rather than simply finding one solution and considering the project finished.

### FDM anisotropic:
Investigate how the Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM) process inherently produces anisotropic parts. Document how this anisotropy may influence the performance and design of your prototype. Additionally, explore strategies to either leverage this behavior to your advantage or mitigate its limitations through design decisions, material selection, or process parameters.

FDM Anisotropy is an important factor to consider when designing and manufacturing engineering prototypes. Printed parts are built layer by layer, their strength, stiffness, and durability can vary depending on print orientation and layer adhesion. This can create weaknesses if the part is not designed around its expected loading conditions.

I can use this knowledge to improve my own prototype by selecting appropriate materials, adjusting print orientation, increasing wall thickness, and optimizing printing parameters. Rather than treating anisotropy only as a limitation, I can use it to my advantage by aligning filament roads with the primary loading directions. By considering these factors before manufacturing, I can produce a stronger, more reliable prototype that better meets its engineering requirements.

### SLA Shrinkage:
Research shrinkage behavior and dimensional accuracy in SLA resin prints, and document how this may affect your prototype’s design and assembly. Include key findings in your portfolio (e.g., typical shrinkage percentages, strategies for compensation, material differences).

SLA printing provides excellent surface quality and dimensional detail, but resin shrinkage can affect the accuracy and assembly of printed components. The amount of shrinkage depends on the resin material, curing conditions, and printing parameters. Understanding these factors allows me to make better decisions when designing and manufacturing my prototype.

I can apply this knowledge by compensating for measured shrinkage in my CAD models, selecting appropriate resin materials, and accounting for tolerances when designing mating components. By testing and measuring printed parts before final assembly, I can reduce dimensional errors and improve the overall fit, alignment, and reliability of my engineering prototype.

### Laser Cut kerf:
Investigate methods for compensating for laser cutting kerf and document how these strategies influence your prototype’s design, tolerances, and assembly. Consider how kerf impacts fit, joint performance, and overall dimensional accuracy, and how design choices can account for these effects.

Laser cutting kerf is an important manufacturing consideration because even a small amount of material removal can affect the dimensions, fit, and performance of assembled components. Without proper compensation, parts may become too small, holes may become too large, and joints may not fit as intended.

I can use this information to improve my prototype by measuring the actual kerf, adjusting CAD dimensions or CAM toolpaths, and including appropriate tolerances in my designs. I would also create test joints before manufacturing the final components to verify that the parts fit correctly. By accounting for kerf early in the design process, I can improve dimensional accuracy, simplify assembly, and create a more reliable engineering prototype.
